Hi. I recently played Black Tower Enigma, a homebrew game for the GameBoy developed by the studio Ogre Pixel. About the same time, I was watching a gameplay of the original Snes realease of Super Mario RPG, and thought: what would the intro of BTE look like if it had been released for the Snes, and animated like Super Mario RPG? And here we go!

I uploaded a version that includes music from the game to my X profile, but prefered not to upload it here to avoid (or reduce at least) copyright problems: https://x.com/changovolcanico/status/2072511988249300999

I made the 3D models of characters based in the collectable pictures that you find in the game (very much like in TLoZ Links Awakening), but trying to also get a look similar to characters in Super Mario RPG

Then I rigged them using Rigify. I started with the Simple Human metarig, then added extra samples for the face, ears, fingers and hair

Stage is based on pixel art in game, while vfx take inspiration from both this game and those in Super Mario RPG.

For the animation render, I used the NLA editor. It took me a while to grasp how it worked, but I felt impressed with the possibility of stacking animations. This allowed me to completely separate the blinking animation, for example, and put it on top and repeat it when needed. Same for talking, that I simply overlaid on top of character's body movement

For the final look, I decided to render in Workbench, as I thought it matched better the look of Super Mario RPG.

On the other hand, my approach to make it look like a Snes game being played on a CRT was as follows.

According to what (I think) I learned in the Youtube channel Displaced Gamers, I rendered in a resolution of 226x224 pixels, as that would match the number of samples in the output of a Snes. Then, I scaled renders by 2 only vertically in the compositor, and multiplied them by a texture with black and white lines to simulate (what we know today as) scanlines, approaching the progressive scan resolution of 480p.

Next, I stretched the obtained images horizontally to match the 4:3 aspect ratio of CRTs (something that in fact occurred when displaying Snes games). The last step was to increase the contrast a little and finally scale by two to obtain a bigger image, easier to show in screens we used nowadays.

I also tested using a 256-color palette, but to be honest, it did not look good. So I left it as is.

And that is it. Thanks for reading!
